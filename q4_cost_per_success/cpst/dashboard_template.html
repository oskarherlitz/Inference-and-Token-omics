What does a finished AI task really cost?

The price per token is like the price of a gallon of gas. What you actually pay for is the cost per mile: each answer that turns out to be right, counting the retries, the failures, and the wrong answers that looked fine. This page measures that for real tasks.

How the measurement works

  1. Run

    Give every model the same real tasks: coding problems, and invoices where every field has to be pulled out exactly.

  2. Check, then grade

    An automatic check (unit tests, or math and source checks for invoices) accepts or rejects each answer, and rejected answers get retried with the reason. Separately, every answer is graded against the true answer, which the model never sees.

  3. Count

    Add up every call, including the failed ones, and divide by the answers that were actually right. That is the cost per correct answer.

The gap between the check and the grade is the point. A cheap model’s mistakes are fine when the check catches them, because a retry costs a fraction of a cent. They are expensive when they slip through, because then they cost you.

To measure your own work, wrap your AI calls with the meter and the same report comes out:

from meter import Meter
meter = Meter("runs/mine.jsonl")
with meter.task("invoice-42") as t:
    resp = client.chat.completions.create(...)
    t.record_openai(resp, model="deepseek-v4-flash", verified=my_check(resp))
print(meter.invoice())
For AI providers: charge per correct answer instead of per token

Today the customer pays for every failed attempt. A provider that measures cost per correct answer can price per success instead, carry the failure risk itself, and keep the savings from smart routing as margin.
